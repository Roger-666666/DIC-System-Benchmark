## 一、从一张塌陷的沙发垫说起

家里那张用了五六年的沙发，坐垫表面看着还完整，坐下去却能感觉中间空了，起身之后凹坑要过一会儿才慢慢回弹。汽车座椅的驾驶位、床垫常年受压的那一侧，走的是同一条路：手感先变软，接着局部凹下去，最后坐着不舒服。这些现象很少是材料断了，多数是海绵反复压缩之后，卸载时没能完全回到原位，留下了一部分永久形变。

工程上把这个残留量叫压缩永久变形（compression set）。表面看只是一个厚度差，实际衡量的是海绵的“记忆力”——卸载之后还能记住多少原来的形状。值低的泡沫长期使用仍能保持形状与回弹力；值高的泡沫舒适性下降、支撑感丧失，产品提前报废。对海绵制品来说，它比初始硬度更接近“寿命”。

也正因为如此，它不是一个用卡尺量一下厚度就能打发的参数。海绵软、多孔、内部结构随机，压缩时表面会鼓、会外扩、会起皱，测量动作本身就可能改变被测对象。先要看清哪里压缩得多、哪里压缩得少，这个数才谈得上测准。

## 二、这个指标在测什么，传统手段给出了什么

压缩永久变形的试验做法很朴素：把试样压缩到规定形变并保持一段时间，卸载、恢复一段时间，再量残留厚度，与原始厚度比出百分比。数值越小，回弹越彻底。整个流程里，读数那一步决定结果的可靠程度，也最容易被海绵“骗”过去。

传统手段常见两类。一类是接触式引伸计，刀口或夹持点贴到试样侧面跟随位移；另一类是直接读试验机压盘行程，把压盘位移当作试样压缩量。两者给出的都是一个数：整体压缩了多少。这个数在批次比对、合格判定、工艺验证的场合很好用，重复性好，操作简单。

它们缺的是空间信息。压盘位移里混着压盘与试样的接触变形、夹具柔度、试样端部的压陷，读到的是“机器走了多远”，不完全等于“试样被压了多少”。刀口贴上去时，附近的泡孔先被压塌，测到的是刀口所在那一小块的局部行为，而这一小块未必是真正的薄弱区。海绵的压缩常从某一处开始——密度偏低、有缺孔、边缘约束较弱的位置——然后塌陷区一点点扩展。整体位移看不见这个先后顺序，也看不见应变集中在哪里。

这不是说传统手段不准，它回答的是另一个问题：整体变形多少。当研究者开始追问“哪一层先塌、塌陷区怎么扩展、有没有局部应变集中”，就需要一张面上的数据，而不只是一条线上的数。

## 三、全场光学设备在测什么

数字图像相关（DIC）的思路是把试样表面当成一张自带纹理的网：先拍一张参考图，压缩过程中连续拍摄，通过追踪表面散斑的移动反算位移，再由位移场求应变场。XTDIC三维应变测量系统做的就是这件事，输出的不是单点读数，而是整个感兴趣区域（ROI）的位移与应变分布。

落到海绵单轴压缩这个场景，设备给出三样结果。

第一样，全场位移。每个计算点都有自己的位移轨迹，表面怎么鼓、横向怎么外扩、边缘怎么内缩，都在这套数据里。70%量级压缩下横向外扩很明显，这一点在压盘位移里体现不出来。

第二样，加载方向（Y向）工程应变全场云图。云图把哪里压得多、哪里压得少直接画出来，应变集中的位置一眼可见。云图上出现条带状的深色区，往往就是泡孔成片坍塌的起点。

第三样，ROI平均应变随加载步的演化曲线。整个区域做平均之后，得到一条平滑、可比对的时间曲线，用来判断阶段、看趋势、做批次间比较。

一台设备同时给“整体一条线”和“表面一张图”，这是它跟传统手段最实质的差别：曲线判断阶段和趋势，云图定位问题、解释曲线为什么这么走。两者互为参照，缺一个就只剩结论。

按加载步看，一次单轴压缩大致走三个阶段。

| 阶段 | 加载步 | 现象 | 应变特征 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 接触调整与线性弹性区 | 0–100 | 压盘贴合试样，泡孔均匀受力 | 应变维持在0附近，小幅波动 |
| 屈服与应力平台起始区 | 100–300 | 局部泡孔开始屈曲 | Y向压缩应变快速达到约-30% |
| 泡孔坍塌平台区（致密化前期） | 300–650 | 泡孔成片坍塌，材料逐渐压实 | 压缩应变接近-70%，曲线速率放缓 |

三段连起来，是从初始接触、泡孔屈曲坍塌到致密化前期的连续测量。第一段平稳说明装夹到位、没有初始预压偏斜；这一段太平或者一上来就掉，通常先怀疑试样没放平。第二段的快速下降是屈服拐点，也是本构模型里最需要标定的位置。第三段那条长而缓的平台直接对应吸能能力——平台越长越平稳，减振垫、防护包装这类应用在整个压缩行程里的力值变化就越可控。

这一款海绵的极限压缩工程应变约-70%。这个数是从云图和曲线上读出来的，不是估算，而且读的时候还能顺带知道：-70%那一刻，哪些区域已经进入致密化，哪些还在平台段。这种“同一时刻不同位置处在不同阶段”的信息，单点手段给不了。

## 四、设备的四条边界

能力要说清楚，边界也要说清楚，否则容易在不合适的试样上反复折腾。

第一条，只测可见表面。相机看到的是朝向镜头的那一面。内部泡孔的坍塌、芯部的密度梯度、夹层结构里层的变形，光学方法给不出来。需要内部信息，得靠断层扫描，或者把试样剖开再测剖面。

第二条，散斑或自然纹理的质量决定数据可用性。表面缺少可追踪的特征就得喷散斑：散斑太大，会跟着泡孔一起变形，失去追踪意义；散斑太小，压缩后糊成一片，匹配直接失效。表面反光强、颜色过深或过浅，都会拉低对比度。

第三条，超大变形需要匹配策略配合，不是任何试样都能一次成功。70%量级的压缩对匹配是硬考验，遇到特别软、侧向容易失稳的试样，可能要调整子区、增加参考帧更新，甚至改变加载方式或加侧向约束。设备有这个能力，不等于每一件试样都一遍过。

第四条，云图色标与参考状态必须统一，否则不可比。同一批试验里，色标范围、参考帧选择、ROI边界都要固定下来，否则两张云图看起来差别很大，实际只是设置不同——这类误判在跨批次对比时特别常见。

知道了能测什么、不能测什么，下一个问题就是：这台设备由哪些部件组成，配置怎么选。

## 五、常见问题

**1. 压缩永久变形和压缩应力应变曲线是一回事吗？**

不是。压缩永久变形是卸载后的残留形变，按原始厚度的百分比表示，说的是回弹能力与使用寿命；应力应变曲线描述的是受载过程中的行为，包括模量、屈服和平台段。海绵可以平台很长而回弹很差，也可以回弹不错但吸能有限。两个数一起看，不能互相替代。

**2. 为什么用双相机而不是单相机？**

单相机测的是面内位移。海绵压到七成时通常还有离面鼓出，曲面会带来透视误差。双相机重建三维坐标，把面外运动分离出来，剩下的才是面内应变。小试样、以面内变形为主时，单相机配远心成像也合理。

**3. 云图上某一点颜色很深，是不是说明那里材料有问题？**

不一定。云图颜色编码的是所选色标范围内的应变大小。颜色深的地方可能是真实的坍塌区，也可能是散斑缺陷、反光，或者ROI跨到了夹持边界。下结论之前先回看那一加载步的原图，确认散斑质量，再检查ROI有没有把夹持区框进去。

**4. 光学测量能替代标准里的厚度测量吗？**

不能替代。标准方法给出的是规定保持与恢复时间之后的单个残留厚度，这个数仍然是判定的依据。全场测量补的是空间信息：坍塌从哪里开始、扩展了多大范围、有没有应变集中。两者回答的问题不同，通常一起报告。

# Why a Sponge's "Memory" Needs a Full-Field Optical System: A Compression-Set Testing Perspective

> **Abstract:** Sagging sofa cushions, collapsed driver's seats and body-shaped hollows in mattresses usually trace back to compression set — the deformation a foam keeps after the load is removed. Contact extensometers and platen travel report a single overall compression value, and on soft foam the probe itself sinks into the specimen, so local collapse stays invisible. An XTDIC three-dimensional strain measurement system reports the displacement field, a Y-direction engineering strain map and a ROI-averaged strain curve from one test, covering contact adjustment, cell buckling and the early densification stage. This article explains what the system measures, how to read the output, and four boundaries of the method.

> **Keywords:** XTDIC; digital image correlation; compression set; foam compression; engineering strain map; full-field strain measurement

## 1. A sagging cushion tells a story

A sofa five or six years old usually keeps an intact surface. Sit down, though, and the middle feels hollow; stand up, and the dent returns slowly and never completely. A driver's seat and the loaded side of a mattress follow the same path: the foam feels softer, a depression forms, sitting becomes uncomfortable.

In most cases the material has not broken. The foam was compressed many times and, when unloaded, did not return to its original position, so part of the deformation stayed behind. That residue is called compression set. On paper it is a thickness difference; in practice it measures a foam's memory — how much of its original shape it recovers. Low compression set keeps shape and resilience through long service; high compression set loses comfort and support and retires the product early. For a foam part, this number sits closer to service life than initial hardness.

That is why a caliper will not settle it. Foam is soft, cellular and structurally random; during compression the surface bulges, spreads and wrinkles, and the act of measuring can change what is measured. Before the number means anything, you need to see where compression is heavy and where it is light.

## 2. What the index measures, and what conventional tools report

The procedure is plain: compress to a specified deformation, hold, release, let it recover, measure the residual thickness and express it as a percentage of the original. A smaller number means a more complete recovery, and everything depends on the reading step.

Two conventional approaches dominate. A contact extensometer puts knife edges or clamping points on the specimen side and follows its movement; a platen reading takes machine travel as specimen compression. Both give one number — how much the specimen compressed overall — which suits batch comparison, screening and process validation, and repeats well.

What they lack is space. Platen travel mixes in contact deformation, fixture compliance and end indentation: it reports how far the machine moved, not how far the specimen compressed. A knife edge crushes the cells beneath it, so the reading describes a small patch that need not be the weak zone. Foam usually starts collapsing at a low-density region, a spot with missing cells or a weakly constrained edge, and the zone spreads from there. Overall displacement shows neither the sequence nor where strain concentrates.

None of this makes the tools inaccurate; they answer a different question. Once the question becomes which layer collapses first and where strain localizes, you need data across a surface rather than a value along a line.

## 3. What a full-field optical system measures

Digital image correlation treats the surface as a texture grid: a reference image is recorded, images are captured during compression, displacement is recovered by tracking speckle movement, and strain follows from the displacement field. An XTDIC three-dimensional strain measurement system works this way and outputs a distribution over a region of interest rather than a single reading.

For uniaxial compression of foam it delivers three results.

The first is the full-field displacement. Every point carries its own trajectory, so bulging, lateral spread and edge drawing-in are all in the dataset; at 70 percent compression, lateral expansion is substantial and platen travel captures none of it.

The second is the engineering strain map in the loading direction (Y), which shows where the specimen is compressed heavily. Banded dark regions usually mark where cells have begun collapsing in groups.

The third is the ROI-averaged strain curve against loading step, a smooth history for judging stage, trend and batch differences.

One system giving both a curve and a map is the real difference from conventional tooling: the curve sets the stage, the map explains why it runs that way.

| Stage | Loading step | What happens | Strain behaviour |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Contact adjustment and linear elasticity | 0–100 | Platen seats, cells load evenly | Strain stays near zero with small fluctuation |
| Yield and start of the stress plateau | 100–300 | Local cells begin to buckle | Y-direction compression reaches about -30% quickly |
| Cell collapse plateau (early densification) | 300–650 | Cells collapse in groups, material compacts | Compression approaches -70%, curve slope decreases |

The three stages cover continuous measurement from first contact through cell buckling to early densification. A flat first stage means the specimen is seated without preload tilt; a stage that starts high or drops at once points to a specimen placed unevenly. The rapid drop in the second stage is the yield knee, the point most worth calibrating in a constitutive model. The long gentle plateau of the third maps onto energy absorption: the longer and flatter it runs, the more controlled the force variation across the stroke, which matters for vibration pads and protective packaging.

The ultimate engineering compression strain here is about -70 percent, read from map and curve rather than estimated, and reading it also shows which regions have entered densification while others remain on the plateau — information no single-point instrument provides.

## 4. Four boundaries of the method

Capabilities need stating with limits, or unsuitable specimens lead to repeated attempts and misplaced doubt.

First, only the visible surface is measured. Internal collapse, core density gradients and inner-layer deformation in a laminate are outside what optics reports; internal information needs computed tomography or a cut section.

Second, speckle or natural texture quality decides whether data is usable. Without trackable features, speckle must be applied: too large and it deforms with the cells, too small and it blurs once compression begins. Strong reflection, or colours too dark or too light, reduce contrast.

Third, very large deformation needs a matching strategy to keep up, and 70 percent compression is a hard test for any matcher: soft specimens that buckle sideways may need adjusted subsets, extra reference updates, or a change of loading or lateral constraint. Having the capability does not mean every specimen succeeds on the first pass.

Fourth, colour scale and reference state must be fixed, or the maps are not comparable. Within one series the colour range, reference frame and ROI boundary all stay the same; otherwise two maps differ only by a setting — a common misreading across batches.

With the measurement range settled, the next question is what the system is made of and how to configure it.

## 5. FAQ

**1. Is compression set the same as a compression stress-strain curve?**

No. Compression set is residual deformation after unloading, given as a percentage of original thickness, and it speaks to recovery and service life. A stress-strain curve describes behaviour under load: modulus, yield, plateau. A foam can hold a long plateau and still recover poorly, or recover well and absorb little. Read them together.

**2. Why use two cameras instead of one?**

A single camera measures in-plane displacement. Foam compressed to 70 percent usually bulges out of plane, and a curved surface introduces perspective error. Two cameras reconstruct three-dimensional coordinates and separate out-of-plane motion from in-plane strain. For small specimens dominated by in-plane deformation, one camera with telecentric imaging is reasonable.

**3. A spot on the map turns red — does that mean the material is defective there?**

Not necessarily. Colour maps encode strain magnitude within the chosen scale. A red patch may be a real collapse zone, a speckle defect, a reflection artefact, or an edge where the ROI crosses a boundary. Before concluding, check the raw image at that step, confirm speckle quality, and verify that the ROI excludes the gripped region.

**4. Does optical measurement replace thickness measurement in the standard?**

No. The standard method produces a single residual thickness after a defined hold and recovery period, and that number remains the basis for acceptance. Optical measurement adds the spatial picture: where collapse started, how far it spread, whether strain concentrated. The two answer different questions and are normally reported together.
